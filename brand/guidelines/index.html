“Because I’m tired of being paperwork for my own work.”
Why Halo exists

Every week, capable people lose hours to writing status updates, sitting in status meetings and grooming tickets. Halo takes that coordination work off their plate. It turns the signal they already create into updates in their own voice, so the work speaks for itself.

Halo · Brand guidelines03
Brand · 02

What we believe

Four principles decide every product and brand call. When two ideas conflict, the one that protects the individual wins.

01
IC-first

Halo speaks for the person, never about them. If a choice makes someone feel watched, it’s wrong, even if a manager would love it.

02
Signal, not paperwork

Halo populates itself from work that already happened. Nobody should maintain a tool so the tool can describe their work.

03
Right altitude

The same truth, written for whoever asks: detail for a teammate, risk for a manager, one sentence for an executive.

04
Visible restraint

Trust comes from showing what was held back. “Redacted: 1 private topic” beats silent omission.

Brand · 04

Voice and tone

Write like a trusted teammate: warm, direct and brief. Halo always speaks in first person on the individual’s behalf.

Sentence case, plain words.
Say “share”, not “report”.
No exclamation marks, no emojis.
Never “monitor”, “track you” or “productivity score”.
Say

Heads up: the selector needs a day more than planned. New target is Thursday.

Not

URGENT!!! Timeline slipping on CHK-142, will circle back ASAP.

Say

4 updates need you. It takes about two minutes.

Not

You have 4 pending items requiring immediate review.

Say

Halo held back 1 update that matches a private topic.

Not

Some content was filtered for compliance reasons.

Say

Rosa asked about Checkout v3. Here’s exactly what Halo said.

Not

Your manager has been monitoring your activity.

Logo · 07

Lockups

Mark and wordmark keep a fixed relationship: the mark is 1.3× the cap height, and the gap is 0.3× the mark.

Horizontal. The default. Use it whenever there is room.
Stacked. For square and tall spaces: app stores, merch, social.
Mark. When Halo is already named nearby: app icon, favicon, avatar.
Wordmark. Only in running text-heavy contexts where the mark repeats elsewhere.

Logo · 09

Color versions

Four versions cover every surface. Pick the one with the strongest contrast; never recolor parts of the mark.

Color on light. The default.
Reversed on Midnight or Ink.
Ink on Signal. Never white on Signal.
White, for single-color and photography.
Halo · Brand guidelines11
Logo · 10

Misuse

The logo only works when it’s exact. These are the mistakes we see most.

Don’t rotate the mark.
Don’t make the ring orange.
Don’t add gradients or glow.
Don’t outline it.
Don’t stretch or squash.
Don’t change the typeface.
Don’t place it on busy imagery.
Don’t add shadows or effects.

Motif system

Four ideas, all drawn from the mark. Use them to build layouts, illustrations and motion.

MC
Boundary

The ring as a container: avatars, progress, frames. It says “this is yours.”

Segment

The Signal arc marks the one thing that matters: progress, attention, live.

Dial

Trust as a spectrum. The segment grows with automation: 45°, 150°, 270°.

Redacted: 1 private topic
Absence

Negative space and redaction bars. Showing what was held back builds trust.
